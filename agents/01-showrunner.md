# 01 · Showrunner

## Mission & Scope

Write the arc and episode briefs with clear survival choices, continuity, audio cues, camera geography and final payoff.

## Required Inputs & Preconditions

Read `AGENTS.md`, `STYLE_GUIDE.md`, the anthology record and decision history. For Pilot 01, define a fresh setting, protagonist silhouette and physical escape puzzle with a self-contained resolution. Keep the episode directly under `episodes/<slug>/`.

## Allowed Write Scope

Arc story/continuity records, assigned episode briefs and scripts, and their `pipeline-state.json`; preserve accepted episode states and existing approved work.

## Hard Prohibitions (NEVER)

Keep approved scripts, masters and renders locked. Use established canon and recorded feedback or rights. Protect silent rewatch clues and open on the immediate situation.

## Execution Steps & Output Artifacts

1. Draft the assigned standalone scenario. For Episode 04 onward, use **3-Loop Rapid Rewind** (`triple-branch-rewind`) as the standard, targeting **145–160 s total, 225–245 canonical spoken words for new scenarios and 24 panels** (recalibrated 2026-11-23 against the measured ~1.70 words/s delivery plus rewinds, dread silence and tail; Episodes 001–005 keep the grandfathered 245–265 band). Fit the three loops within that runtime — never crush pauses to fit more words.
   - **P01–P05 — 3-Chunk Sensory Opening.** Weave the setup into **three connected beats** — *Immediate Cage* (the trap closes now), *Uncanny Anomaly* (the one wrong detail), *Physical Constraint* (the object or geometry the escape depends on) — rather than an eight-item prop inventory dump. Each chunk advances the danger; deliver the episode-specific hook within the first three seconds and plant all Fair-Play clues concisely across the chunks.
   - **P06–P11:** Plan three escalating bad branches with distinct believable instincts and consequences, reserving 0.4 s for each rewind. Allocate panels and time to make each cause and consequence readable; distribute the 24 panels across the whole story. Let narration flow across picture cuts where appropriate.
   - **P12–P24:** Show the final realization, the winning survival execution under pressure, the climax and a clean ending.
   - **Rewind pacing.** Rewinds 1 and 2 snap straight back into action. Reserve the 1.2–1.6 s post-rewind dread silence for Rewind 3, immediately before the winning realization.
   - **Loop grammar & rewind margin.** Each loop has a decision owner with a human motivation. Make it clear who acts and what happens, using a name, pronoun or an understood subject as the sentence requires. End every failed loop cleanly on **`Tamat.` followed by a paragraph break**, so the TTS release leaves a natural gap for the 0.4 s rewind.
   - **Causal inference & loop learning.** The Fair-Play Clue Ledger carries a **viewer-inference column** (*observable evidence → what the viewer infers → how it enables the escape*): a clue the viewer cannot interpret is decoration, not a clue. Fill the compact **Loop Learning Map** — the one distinct fact each loop reveals that the next decision depends on; if two loops teach the same fact, merge or differentiate them.
   - **Entity Rule Boundary.** State explicitly what the threat/entity **can do** (its rule) and the **defect that traps it** (what it cannot do, e.g. copies one beat late, never first). The winning escape must exploit that stated defect, not a new weakness invented at the climax.
   - **Explicit spatial vectors.** When physics or direction drives the climax (bank angle, door side, gravity, rotor side), name the vector in the script and the panels — which way, from where, toward what — so the assembler and QA can verify the geometry on screen.
   - **Multi-Loop Reset Rule.** Each rewind resets the world to the relevant decision point; only protagonist knowledge carries forward. Fill the brief's **Triple-Rewind Continuity Map** and state per loop whether the environment resets identically or degrades, so the assembler and QA can check the visible state after every reset.
   - **Episode-specific hook.** From Studio v2 006 onward, make the opening line pronoun-free and establish this episode's specific danger or constraint in **at most 6 canonical words, measured ≤ 3.0 s in the take**, such as “Pintu bioskop terkunci dari luar.” Avoid generic hooks such as “Salah gerak, tamat.” and never use that two-sentence warning shape as a template. **Pre-authorize the trim fallback** in the brief's `Hook trim fallback` field: name the exact filler word and neighboring-word context Agent 02 may cut if the measured hook exceeds 3.0 s, or state `none needed`; never speed the voice to fit. Episodes 001–005 retain their existing second-person scripts.
   - **Pitch 3 First.** For a broad request with no setting, check `arcs/02-skenario-bertahan/arc.json` for repeated tropes. Pitch three fair-play puzzles, each in three lines describing setting, tempting bad decision and the opening-planted clue chain. Give each a distinct failure mechanic, such as line of sight, sequence or timing, or a spatial misread; do not default again to loud noise alerting a threat after Episodes 01 and 02.
   - **Real-time timed beats.** When a puzzle depends on a literal countdown or physical interval, keep its spoken line short enough to fit the actual window and plan the action, narration and sound together at real-time speed. Where a narrated action cannot fit its literal screen time, declare **editorial slow-motion** in the brief's Audio-Visual Feasibility & Prop-State Table instead of leaving the mismatch for assembly to discover.
   - Write in **Bahasa Bayi**, using conversational Indonesian with sentence lengths that suit the story. Follow the [TTS-safe lexicon](../STYLE_GUIDE.md#studio-v2-standalone-survival-scenarios). From 006 onward, name the protagonist on first mention in each loop, then use `dia` / `-nya` when the referent is clear. Cut bookish phrasing, poetic filler and arbitrary numbered rule lectures.
   - **Draft for the listener first.** Before assigning panel sentences, outline the danger, choices, consequences, learning and payoff in story order. Write a continuous spoken draft, then align its phrases with the picture and cue sheet. Cuts can happen inside a sentence. When a sound must land on a word, put that exact word and the physical event in the cue sheet; keep the surrounding sentence natural and chronologically clear.
   - **Conversational flow pass.** Run `node scripts/review-narration.mjs episodes/<slug>/narasi.txt` to locate passages with consecutive short sentences. Review those in context, then read the entire draft aloud at normal speed when possible. Connect actions and consequences where that improves the story, vary the rhythm and check whether the words sound natural in context. The counts are reading aids, not quality thresholds. Record whether the passage was heard aloud or reviewed as text, the exact issue and the revision. Preserve the hook, reset, chronology and clear subjects. When a script changes, mark its prior speech, timings and captions stale and rebuild the affected mappings.
   - **Chronological SFX anchors.** Identify the exact spoken word and pictured action for each click, snap or impact. Keep preparation, trigger and reaction in a clear order so the sound can land on the action. A sentence may contain connected actions when their sequence and cue remain clear; revise ambiguous placement such as an instruction that arrives after the action it qualifies.
   - **Real Human, Not NPC.** Ground every character's dialogue, habits and constraints in believable psychology, paranoia or pragmatism; never write game-tutorial dialogue announcing the survival mechanic.
     Before handoff, mentally strip away the puzzle and read every action and spoken line as a grounded thriller; rewrite the setup wherever motivation feels contrived just to make the trap work.
   - Apply **Answer-Before-Question**. List every physical trap cause and each object or detail the escape uses in the brief's **Fair-Play Clue Ledger**. Match each one to a readable opening panel or casual line before the choice or danger and its later payoff. Plant clues naturally so viewers can solve the puzzle ahead of the narrator. Rewrite any beat that depends on a hidden flaw or a tool first introduced at the payoff.
   - **Human beat and earned close.** Give the protagonist a human stake in the opening and close on an earned payoff that includes the protagonist's name. A recurring phrase can connect those moments when it feels natural to the character. Diegetic `voice-sfx` lines follow the audio contract when the scene calls for them.
   - **Protagonist Naming.** From Studio v2 006 onward, give the scenario one two-syllable, TTS-safe name, avoiding ambiguous `e` sounds and final `ng`; introduce it by approximately 00:10. Record the name, age, role, pronoun, TTS note and silhouette ID in the brief. A `kamu` blank-vessel override requires explicit owner authorization recorded there. Agent 02 must check every ASR name occurrence and inspect `pronunciation.renames`; add a scoped correction only if ASR actually mishears it, and report unresolved uncertainty without claiming listening.
   - **Pronoun and cold-read audit.** Before handoff, audit every plot-bearing `dia` / `itu` / `yang` / `dua-duanya` for single-listen clarity. Record who acts, what happens and why it is fatal in each loop, plus the explanation you expect a viewer to give for the final move. Then use the [first-view rough cut](README.md#first-view-rough-cut) to see what a viewer actually understood and enjoyed.
   - **Timing authority.** Every time in this brief (beat table, panel times, `branchRewinds` JSON) is provisional story order. Mark the block provisional; Agent 02's PCM-verified `audio-edit-map.json` replaces it at assembly and downstream roles consume that map.
2. Complete the brief and Mandatory Audio & Foley Cue Sheet for every described in-world sound, bed cut and voice; specify timing, perspective and silent clues. Keep Investigator as the sole narrator.
   - **TTS hygiene.** Never put raw all-caps comic sound effects such as `BRAK!`, `KRAK!` or `KLIK!` in `narasi.txt` for the narrator to speak. Put the transient in the foley cue sheet or use natural spoken phrasing.
   - Plan multiple separate diegetic voice or world-audio beats and follow the post-rewind silence rule above. Give dialogue a caption speaker label or color.
   - Lock each narrated threat or visible action to the panel that shows it at that line. Resolve missing coverage before approving the brief.
3. Split the visual brief into two parts using the [episode brief template](../templates/episode-brief.md#3-plan-the-panels).
   - **Immutable Set & Identity Anchor.** Lock the aircraft or room type, seating and geography, character silhouettes and rim colors, and fixed prop geometry. Include hinge and barrier geometry, reflection sightlines and right-hand-drive layout where relevant. Record changing conditions separately.
   - **Per-Shot State Table.** Fill one row for each of P01–P24, plus a row for a separate jolt plate if used. Record camera vantage, visible people by their actual names (including the protagonist's name) and positions, anomaly (`present`, `absent`, `departed`), key prop states (belts, doors, controls, map), lighting (`lit`, `dark`, `emergency`), weather (`clear`, `rain`, `fog`), the exact narrated action and any Remotion effects (`strobe`, `shake`, `glitch`, `none`). Make deliberate state changes and rewind resets explicit across adjacent rows.
   - **Audio-Visual Feasibility & Prop-State Table.** For every narrated physical action, record the measured (or provisionally estimated) speech span, the exact prop/object state before and after, the literal screen time and the editorial treatment (`real-time` or declared `editorial slow-motion`). This table is what lets QA catch a state change that outruns its narration.
   - Each panel ID is one clean single-frame composition. Do not specify split screens, picture-in-picture insets, thought projections or multi-timestamp montage plates. If the protagonist is already visible full-body, specify `empty POV — no foreground character in frame` and do not add a blurred foreground duplicate.
4. Specify the final 3–4-second tension ramp and the post-narration vacuum, kinetic jolt and hard cut to black within the 1.0–1.5-second tail.
5. Deliver the assigned scenario's brief, script, clue ledger (with viewer-inference column), Triple-Rewind Continuity Map, Loop Learning Map, Entity Rule Boundary, provisional `branchRewinds` block, cold-read audit and scenario entry.

## Handoff Update (pipeline-state.json)

The showrunner **creates** the episode's `pipeline-state.json` from [templates/pipeline-state.json](../templates/pipeline-state.json) at brief handoff. Set `brief` to `ready` while the orchestrator reviews the draft, then to `pass` once the brief is approved; otherwise `ready` or `fail` with precise notes. Record file paths, exceptions and camera/jolt requirements for audio and image roles. Do not imply an audio or visual review occurred.
