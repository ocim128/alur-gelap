## Standalone scenario

- **Collection:** [Skenario Bertahan](../../arcs/02-skenario-bertahan/arc.json), Pilot 01 only. Independent Indonesian kos setting, protagonist and puzzle; no Arc 01 characters, outbreak or continuity.
- **Premise:** A captive has to reach a high window without alerting the captor sitting three metres away. Dragging the heavy chair is fast but loud; lifting it onto a towel is quieter. Before the next check she must restore the chair and the towel's blue corner, leaving the window closed even though she has released its latch.
- **Opening state:** Night in a locked ground-floor room. She is unrestrained. Available items shown in the opening: heavy wooden chair, chalk square, blue-corner towel, ceramic floor, high window, exterior drum and left-hand alley. A ticking digital clock HUD establishes the check cycle. The captor carries the existing key and knife. There is no phone, hidden tool, helper or alternative exit.

She has the next three-minute interval to spread the towel again, lift the chair beneath the window, push the already-unlatched window outward, lower herself onto the planted drum and escape left to the street. The door stays locked. She leaves the chair under the open window after she has escaped; there is no claim that the room remains undisturbed at the next check.

## Spatial & Camera Layout

**Room and walkway.** A dry 3 m × 3 m ground-floor kos utility room with hard ceramic tile. The north wall contains a locked wooden door and high window. Outside that wall is a narrow covered walkway. From inside facing north, the walkway leads **left/west** to an alley and the street; the captor walks **right/east** to a low concrete step at a short corner approximately 3 m from the door and within earshot of scraping. P04 shows him seated at that step, with the same route, corner and drum in one exterior frame. No other people or exits are introduced. Falling rain is visible only outdoors beyond the walkway roof, never in the room or through a covered ceiling.

**Door and check.** The wooden door is 0.85 m wide and approximately 2 m high; hinges are on the west/left jamb viewed from the room, and it opens **inward** after the exterior deadbolt is unlocked with the captor's key. The captive cannot reach that lock from inside. A hand-sized sliding hatch at approximately 1.4 m height lets him stoop and look into the room at the captive and chair; the 2 m-high window sill is above the door on the same wall. A small bottom gap admits a narrow stripe of exterior light so his approaching shoes cast an interruption. The door has no bell or cord. During the survival branch the hatch opens for the check but the door remains shut and locked.

**Window and descent.** An unbarred, clear 0.8 m × 0.7 m side-hinged window sits directly above the door, sill at 2 m. Its right/east vertical edge is hinged and it opens outward toward the covered walkway. The stiff inside latch at its left edge is visible in P06; the sash and snug seal initially sit **closed and flush** in the frame. From the chair she can reach and quietly release the latch while holding the sash shut; after release the snug sash remains seated with no outward projection. No breeze dislodges it during the check. The latch is inside and above the hatch, out of the guard's view through the hatch; the flush exterior looks unchanged from the walkway. After he leaves she pushes the same unlatched sash fully outward.

A stable, closed, non-rolling drum sits outside just to the right of the door, below the window's right edge; its top is about 0.85 m above the walkway. It leaves the hatch and doorway clear. From the chair she gets onto the sill, sits and turns her feet outward, lowers one foot to the drum and then steps onto the walkway. The window opening, drum and left route are planted in the opening. No rope or extra equipment is used.

**Chair and towel.** A solid, roughly 7–8 kg wooden chair with four bare feet begins inside a white chalk square on dry ceramic, to the east/right of the door and roughly one metre from the position beneath the window. A large, folded towel, at least 1.1 m square when opened, rests on its seat; its distinctive blue corner faces the door. P05 shows the towel, chair, chalk and floor together. The captor's “Jangan geser apa pun” establishes that changed placement risks discovery. The frame must show four chair feet fitting on the spread towel. She removes and spreads the towel on the floor first, lifts the chair clear of the tiles with both hands, carries it to the window and lowers all four feet onto the fabric; the towel cushions landing contact. She does not hold the chair in one hand while laying out the towel. Careful lifting also avoids the dragging scrape. No claim of guaranteed silence: placement, floor conditions and the latch are performed deliberately and need review.

At the `09:03` check she lifts the chair back onto the original chalk square, folds the towel and nearly leaves its blue corner facing the wall. She corrects it to face the door before the hatch opens. The sash stays closed and flush throughout. Once the captor leaves, she spreads the towel and carries the chair to the window again; she need not restore either after leaving the room.

**Sightlines and continuity.** Interior camera positions use the south wall looking north unless a panel says otherwise. Exterior shots use the same north wall on the covered walkway. The captor's opening checks are shown in two **separate panels** with live Remotion digital clock HUD displays reading `08:57` and `09:00` (no wall clocks painted into plates); between them the edit explicitly compresses three in-world minutes. Another indicated time jump brings the decision to `09:01:50`. The next routine check is `09:03`. In the bad branch he hears the scrape and returns early from his established position. The 0.4 s rewind reverses the digital clock HUD back to the same `09:01:50` choice: chair in square, towel folded, sash shut and latched, captive unharmed. In the surviving branch, the roughly 70 seconds to the scheduled check cover preparation and reset; no unmarked extra minutes are introduced. Panel times are estimates until measured audio exists.

## Script, decision and fair play

**Opening:** “Kamu dikunci di kamar. Salah gerak, tamat.” The locked door and trapped survivor appear immediately. The voice is simple second-person Indonesian; [narasi.txt](narasi.txt) contains the single-speaker narration only.

**Bad branch:** Because she needs height and the chair is heavy, sliding it toward the window feels fast. The wooden feet scrape hard ceramic; the nearby captor hears, runs back, unlocks the existing door and stabs her with the established knife. The red/amber sequence visibly ends with her collapsed and motionless. A **0.4 s `branchRewind`** resets the choice at approximately **00:59.6–01:00.0**; this death is not the surviving outcome.

**Survival branch:** Spread the visible towel, lift instead of slide, place the chair on the towel, reach the window and work only the stiff inside latch. Keep the sash fully closed and flush. At the approaching check restore the chalk placement, folded towel and blue corner, then sit in the observed position. He sees the expected room through the hatch, closes it and goes right. Now push the unlatched sash outward and take the pre-established drum and left route.
