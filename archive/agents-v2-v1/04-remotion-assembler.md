## Archived · Studio v2 / v1 Assembler contract (Episodes 001–005 & Arc 01)

> Archived on 2026-11-24. Preserved for existing `StudioV2Player` and Arc 01 compositions only; do not apply narrator/panel/jolt assembly rules to Studio v3 episodes.

1. Assemble the owner-accepted narration and approved panels into a 9:16 manifest/composition, finalizing provisional caption and cue timing against the available sidecars and footage. Keep title safe-zone timing, captions of 1–4 words (aim for 3–4 when boundaries allow), HUD numbers and silent clues correct; ensure visible character reactions and purposeful motion.
   - **Single Timing Authority.** The brief's timings are provisional story order; the PCM-verified `audio-edit-map.json` is the composition timeline authority. Re-anchor every provisional frame number (including the brief's `branchRewinds` block) to the measured map — for tape snaps use `assemblerHandoff.rewindSnapAnchors` and derive sample offsets as `Math.round(mappedSeconds * sampleRate)` — and set manifest `timing.*`, captions, cuts and mix cues from `audio-edit-map.json` plus `narration-timestamps.json`. Never reconcile a mismatch by editing prose or sidecars: a drift fails the validator and the export verifier loudly.
2. Implement the final **tension ramp → silence vacuum → camera-shake/flash kinetic jolt → hard cut to black** per `STYLE_GUIDE.md`. Preserve cue-sheet foley and any episode-specific final handoff; do not speed up narration.
3. Target **−14.0 LUFS integrated** and **≤ −1.5 dBTP** for the complete v2 mix; mix climax SFX peaks within **−8 to −2 dB relative to narration peaks**, then measure the compressed delivery's true peak. Keep the impact crisp without clipping.
4. Run `npm run check`, render the MP4 and export verification stills including 00:00.2, representative HUD/face frames and final jolt/black. For a standard render use `npm run render -- <composition-id> <delivery.mp4> [Remotion options]`: it renders and automatically applies the ShortZ balanced preset. When a separate mastering/remux step is needed, finish it first and run `npm run compress -- <master.mp4>`; this creates a neighboring `_compressed.mp4` without overwriting an existing file. Deliver the compressed MP4, manifest, captions, composition ID, duration, mix measurements, rights and verification evidence. Retain a separately mastered source when needed for revisions; remove only confirmed disposable intermediates after successful delivery, never approved cuts or the only master.

- **Foley variation.** Avoid repeating the exact same breath or exhale sample across consecutive narration gaps. Alternate subtle variations or duck repeated breaths by 4–6 dB so the loop does not stand out on headphones.
- **Caption hygiene.** Strip only stray non-word TTS punctuation artifacts before the final render. Preserve valid Indonesian reduplication hyphens (`satu-satunya`, `pelan-pelan`, `keras-keras`) and standard prefixes (`dihantam`); never mechanically remove hyphens or rewrite spoken words.
- **Speaker labels.** Hide the `NARATOR` / `NARRATOR` badge and use white caption words by default, yellow for key clues/actions and red for threats. Display a colored label for non-narrator diegetic voices, such as yellow `PENCULIK`.

### Studio v2 assembly contract

For Studio v2 Scenario 03 (`episodes/003-goyangan-dek-enam`) onward, import `StudioV2Player` from `src/StudioV2Player.tsx` instead of writing another player. Register a composition whose duration matches `manifest.duration`, and pass `<StudioV2Player manifest={manifest} captions={captions} />`. The player accepts ordered `scenes: [{id, at, plate, camera}]`, `soundMix: {asset, gain?}`, and `timing: {blackoutFrame, rewindFrame?, rewindFrames?}`. Each scene requires a camera with finite `from` and `to` coordinates and positive scales; at least one of `x`, `y` or `scale` must change. Its movement spans the entire scene, including holds longer than 9 seconds. New Studio v2 builders should omit Arc 1 `reframes`, `characters`, `hud` and `beds`; older manifests retain them as historical metadata. The movement check does not judge whether a slow push or long hold feels purposeful; review pacing and motion in the actual export. For a `tactical-hud` editorial purpose, show and review the diagram or guide in the composition; Studio v2 overlays cannot be verified from Arc 1 HUD metadata. Frames are composition-relative; mix and plate assets are public-relative. It selects plates, animates camera drift, plays the prepared mix, shows lower-third captions, applies the default 0.4 s rewind when scheduled, and blacks out picture and captions at `blackoutFrame` while audio continues to the composition end.

Use the manifest as the timing source of truth: register `fps` and `durationInFrames` from its `fps` and `duration`; store each named event once as an integer `timing.*Frame`. Reference a cue-derived scene with `atCue` and set its `at` from that cue in the builder; validation rejects drift. Record `soundMix.sampleRate` and derive audio sample positions as `round(frame * sampleRate / fps)` plus any explicit integer `*SampleOffset` for sub-frame alignment. Builders, mixers and export verifiers must read those same fields; verification detects events independently in the encoded MP4. See `episodes/002-rumah-tengah-hutan`'s manifest, builder, mix and verifier for the pattern.

Captions are `{startMs, endMs, speaker?, words: [{text, color?, startMs?, endMs?}]}` on the same composition timeline. A chunk that crosses a scene cut **must** carry measured `startMs`/`endMs` for each word. The player assigns a word spanning a cut only to the panel with the longest overlap (earlier panel on a tie), so it is never duplicated; it fails rather than guessing missing word timings. `scripts/caption_helpers.py` provides `build_caption_beats(words, cuts, fps, color_overrides)` for timed narrator words, the one-panel rule and semantic-color defaults for Python caption builders. It groups 1–4 tokens per beat, splitting at gaps over six frames, scene cuts, sentence ends and ASR chunk changes; each token retains its measured input interval. Normalize episode-specific spelling before calling it. Review and adjust colors against the actual story, and use optional per-episode `caption-colors.json` overrides where needed. Use `plateOverlay={(context) => ...}` for scene-locked hatch or mask effects and `overlay={(context) => ...}` for screen-space rain, lightning or titles. Both receive `{frame, fps, scene, sceneIndex, sceneEndFrame}` in composition time. Verify the cut and final compressed mix; the player does not create ambience, sound effects or episode-specific overlays. Preserve the existing Pilot 01 (`episodes/001-kamar-terkunci`) and Scenario 02 (`episodes/002-rumah-tengah-hutan`) compositions and exports.

Reusable components are exported from `src/components/StudioV2.tsx`. Import the named exports directly. Frame props use the **local frame of the containing composition or Sequence**. Put matching visual and audio cues on the same timeline. Normalized boxes use `{x, y, width, height}` in 0–1 composition coordinates. `FrameTransition` is `{at: number; duration: number; from: number; to: number}`; provide transitions sorted by `at` without overlap.

| Component | Props and behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `SilhouetteAtmosphereLayer` | `{grainOpacity?: number; grainSeed?: string; flicker?: {box: NormalizedBox; color: string; opacity?: number; strength?: number}}`. Grain and optional practical-light flicker are deterministic per frame. Use distinct seeds for multiple layers. |
| `FlashOverlay` | `{startFrame: number; durationFrames?: number; opacity?: number; color?: string}`. Defaults to three frames and 60% opacity, capped at 65%. Its opacity cap does not guarantee output luma; measure the composited export. |
| `BranchRewindTransition` | `{startFrame: number; durationFrames?: number; intensity?: number; seed?: string}`. The overlay lasts 0.4 s by default at the composition FPS. Place the reverse-whoosh/tape-stop sound separately at the same frame. |
| `LiveDigitalClockHUD` | `{initialSeconds: number; jumps?: {atFrame: number; seconds: number}[]; rewind?: {startFrame: number; durationFrames: number; fromSeconds: number; toSeconds: number}; showSeconds?: boolean; warning?: boolean; box?: NormalizedBox; fontSize?: number}`. Seconds are time-of-day values. The clock ticks using composition FPS, applies scheduled jumps, reverses during the supplied interval, then resumes from the rewind target. `showSeconds` selects HH:MM:SS; `warning` selects red. |
| `SlidingHatchOverlay` | `{box: NormalizedBox; initialOpen?: number; transitions: FrameTransition[]; direction?: "left" \| "right" \| "up" \| "down"; color?: string}`. | `UnderDoorShoeShadow` | `{box: NormalizedBox; startFrame: number; stopFrame: number; fromX?: number; color?: string}`. Two matte blocks enter the light strip and halt centered at `stopFrame`. `fromX` is relative to the strip width and defaults to −0.3. |

The lateral push and decaying jitter start at the contact frame; defaults are 10 frames, 5 px jitter and 8 px push. Schedule the scrape or impact SFX at that same frame. |

Record `pipelineVersion: 2` separately from `schemaVersion: 2`. Use `art.styleId: "urban-silhouette-noir"` and `script.format: "decision-consequence"`. Set `script.engine` to `triple-branch-rewind` for Episode 04 onward. Keep the frame-zero hook and timed ending/reveal. Record the spoken count in `script.wordCount`.

For the three-loop format, declare exactly three `timing.branchRewinds` entries as `{startFrame, endFrame}`. The player draws one tape-snap/glitch transition per interval. Use inclusive starts and exclusive ends with `endFrame = startFrame + Math.round(0.4 * fps)` (12 frames at 30 fps) and finish all three by blackout. Use the array in place of `timing.rewindFrame` and `timing.rewindFrames`, which still drive single-rewind cuts.

Place the three tape-snap SFX in the prepared mix at the corresponding start frames using `Math.round(startFrame * sampleRate / fps)`. Cut back to the established choice point as each transition resolves. Rewinds 1 and 2 return immediately to action. Reserve the 1.2–1.6 s post-rewind dread silence for Rewind 3 before the winning realization. Verify all three visual intervals and audio cues in the compressed export.

- **Continuous 2.5D motion and atmosphere.** Animate every plate throughout its hold with a subtle push, pull or pan (for example, 1.00 → 1.04); no frozen slides. Make procedural rain or mist visibly move on outdoor or window-visible shots, and make scripted high-contrast lightning flashes 2–3 frames long and legible at phone size. Keep falling rain outside or behind glass; preserve intentional dry interiors. Use deterministic grain and practical light flicker where appropriate, and check the actual export at phone size.
- **Flash cap and color.** Use `FlashOverlay` or an equivalent overlay at about 55–65% opacity, capped at 65%; measure the actual exported impact frame's mean luma and reduce the overlay until it is below 190 on an 8-bit scale. Keep shadow detail visible. Deliver Rec.709 limited-range `yuv420p` video with matching BT.709 color tags; check the compressed MP4 with `ffprobe`.
- **`branchRewind`.** For single-rewind cuts, use a 0.35–0.55 s scanline tear/static snap with reverse whoosh or tape-stop audio. Anchor each rewind transient to its measured narration cue.
- **Living ambience.** Maintain a continuous, evolving ambient bed, cleanly ducked beneath narration; only intentional scripted Dread Silences drop to true zero. Preserve the prescribed final silence vacuum.
- **Caption safe zone and cuts.** Place burned-in captions in the lower-third safe zone, approximately 76–82% down the frame, clear of the TikTok/Reels bottom UI and central 60% of the artwork. Split or clamp caption chunks at every panel cut so no caption hangs frozen across it; preserve spoken-word timing and coverage when the sentence continues on the next shot. Verify in the exported cut at phone size.
- **Picture and sound.** Check the Show-What-You-Say map against the cut before export. Keep each action or threat on screen during its narration. Mix multiple world-audio or dialogue beats and preserve 1–3 s dread silences. Use speaker labels and semantic word colors for dialogue captions. Keep `tensionRamp`, `finalJolt`, −14.0 LUFS, ≤ −1.5 dBTP and the ±1-frame exported PCM sync check.

### Audio timestamps and exported jolt sync

- Anchor story-critical SFX transients, including impacts, thunderclaps, door slams and `branchRewind`, to the exact measured word timestamps from Agent 02's accepted timing sidecar, rather than panel start frames. Apply any story-required offset from that word explicitly (for example, a delayed thunderclap); verify the event, exported waveform and visuals agree. If word timing is missing or uncertain, resolve it before handoff rather than guessing from the panel cut.
- Inspect narration and SFX timestamps before the final mix and mux. When an input has a PTS discontinuity, build a working PCM copy with continuous timestamps derived from its sample count. Preserve intentional pauses and cue placement on the composition timeline. Verify the normalized input before mixing.
- Compression re-encodes audio to AAC, which can change loudness, onset alignment and digital silence. Perform the export sync, true-peak, silence and visual checks on the **compressed delivery MP4**, not just its master. Document any codec residue; never claim exact zero without measuring it.
- Decode the AAC in the final exported MP4 to PCM and locate the stinger onset in the waveform. Read the presentation timestamp of the first visual `finalJolt` flash frame from the exported video. Compare both on the MP4 presentation timeline, accounting for stream offsets and AAC priming or trim. Require an absolute offset of **at most one frame (approximately ±33 ms at 30 fps)**. Record the frame number, both timestamps and the signed offset in the handoff. If the limit is exceeded, correct the timing, export again and repeat the measurement before sending the cut to Agent 05.
- For Studio v2 Scenario 02 (`episodes/002-rumah-tengah-hutan`), run `python episodes/002-rumah-tengah-hutan/verify-export.py <delivery.mp4> [report.json]`; a nonzero exit blocks handoff. Its wrong-video, offset and non-finite regressions run with `python episodes/002-rumah-tengah-hutan/test_verify_export.py`. The verifier reports silence levels rather than claiming AAC is exactly silent. New deliveries should build on the shared helpers in `scripts/verify_export_common.py`: reports must not overwrite an existing report, must bind the target MP4's filename and SHA-256, must scan the surrounding frame interval (not just the scheduled frame) for flash/jolt/luma transitions, and should auto-export short listening-check clips around splices, dialogue overlaps, rewinds and the final word-to-stinger tail.
